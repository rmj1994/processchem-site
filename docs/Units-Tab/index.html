Units Tab

About

Choose the unit for each quantity separately, or set them all at once from a preset. There is no single SI/Imperial switch, because engineers mix bar with °C and psi with °F.

Changing a unit converts what you have already typed rather than reinterpreting it. This holds on every tab: a value typed as 20 bar on the Property tab becomes 290.08 psi if you switch pressure to psi, not 20 psi. Any calculation showing a result goes back to its inputs, because that result was calculated in the old units; tap Calculate again for an up-to-date one. The Docs ↗ pill in the header opens this page.

Presets

The row of chips at the top sets every quantity at once: Process (default), Strict SI, Metric, British and US. The row scrolls sideways to show them all. The chip of the preset your choices match is highlighted; change any one quantity afterwards and no chip is, since the mix is no longer that preset.

A preset is only a starting point: any quantity can still be changed on its own afterwards.

Choosing a unit

The quantities are grouped in cards: Temperature & pressure, Amount & flow, Energy & heat, Fluid properties, and Size, time & velocity. A folded card lists the units in use; tap its heading to open it. Each quantity is a row with its name, how many units it offers, and the unit in use. Tap the unit to choose another.

The search box, "Find a quantity or unit, e.g. psi", finds a quantity by its name or by any of its units, and opens every card with a match.

A few of these deserve a note. barg and psig are gauge pressures, an offset from atmospheric rather than a scale factor, so 0 barg is 1.01325 bara. Pressure drop is separate from Pressure because a drop has no gauge form, and its natural size, a fluidized bed's or a distributor plate's, is far smaller than the pressure it is measured against. Velocity is a gas velocity, such as a fluidized bed's superficial, minimum-fluidization and terminal velocities.

Where a quantity can be shown per unit mass or per mole (specific heat capacity, and the enthalpy and Gibbs energy of formation), the app needs the chemical's molecular weight to switch between the two, and takes it from the chemical you are looking at.

The defaults here and the units on each field

What you choose here is the default for the whole app. Each number field on the other tabs also has its own unit menu, and each result value can be tapped to show it in another unit. A field follows this tab until you pick a unit in the field itself; from then on that field keeps its own unit, and later changes here leave it alone. A unit picked on a result value applies to that one value while the app is open. Neither ever changes the choices on this tab.

Normal reference

The Normal reference card, at the foot of the tab, sets the temperature and pressure that normal flows are measured at; its heading shows the current reference. It defaults to 20 °C and 1.01325 bara (1 atm) and is worth checking against your instrument: "normal" conditions are not standardised, and a 0 °C reference reads about 7 % higher in mass terms. DIN 1343 uses 0 °C, while standard-litre mass flow controllers commonly use 20 °C or 25 °C.

Tap the card to open it and type the Temperature (°C) and Pressure (bara). The value is committed when you leave the field, not on every keystroke, so a half-typed number never becomes the live reference. A pressure at or below zero, or a temperature at or below absolute zero, is refused with a message and the old reference stays in force.

NL/min, NL/h, Nm³/h, Nm³/min, Nm³/d, Nft³/h and Nft³/min are all measured at your reference. Sm³/h, SCFM and MMSCFD are the exception: they keep their own standards (Sm³ is ISO 13443 at 15 °C and 101.325 kPa; SCFM and MMSCFD, million standard cubic feet per day, are at 60 °F and 14.696 psia) because that is what those names mean on an instrument or a pipeline. Selecting one puts a second reference state in play next to yours, and the conversion between the two is applied for you.

Every place a normal flow can be entered (Phase Equilibrium, Reaction, Heater, Heat Exchanger, Fluidized Bed) uses this one reference, and each says which reference is in force.

Unit converter

Unit converter, under the search box, opens a converter sheet. Choose a quantity from the chips along the top, type a value, and pick the unit it is in from the chips under it. The value appears at once in every other unit of that quantity, and changes as you type. It starts on pressure, in the unit chosen on this tab.

It is independent of the tab's own choices. Looking a figure up from a datasheet never changes the units the rest of the app displays in.

Quantities whose units need a chemical's molecular weight to convert (specific heat capacity, and the enthalpy and Gibbs energy of formation) are left out, because a general converter has no substance to take the weight from.

Saving your choices

The ⋯ menu in the header saves and opens your unit choices. Save… stores every unit choice and the Normal reference together under a name of up to 40 characters, up to 25 sets, either In the app or in Files / iCloud as a file; saving under an existing name replaces it. Sets saved in the app are listed under Recent at the top of the menu, and Open a saved set… lists them all. The set you last saved or opened comes back when the app starts, and units are restored before any other tab so the values on those tabs land in the right units. Reset to defaults returns every quantity to the Process (default) preset and the reference to 20 °C and 1 atm, and leaves your saved sets untouched. Sets saved in the app are deleted with it, so keep a file of anything you want to keep; see Saving and opening.
