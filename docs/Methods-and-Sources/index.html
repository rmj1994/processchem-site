Methods and Sources

About

Every number ProcessChem shows can tell you where it came from: the ⓘ beside a Property value opens its data source, and the Methods used row at the foot of a result lists the methods behind it. Where more than one method exists, you can pick another and the result is calculated again with it.

Where the data come from

  • CoolProp reference equations of state, the most accurate available, for the 100-plus pure fluids CoolProp covers: water, the common gases, light hydrocarbons, refrigerants and more.
  • Peng-Robinson cubic equation of state for other fluids, and for mixtures, with fitted binary interaction parameters from ChemSep.
  • thermo / chemicals (Caleb Bell's libraries) for property correlations fitted to measured data, drawn from DIPPR, Perry's and the literature.
  • NASA CEA thermodynamic data for 1,820 species, used where the other libraries have none.
  • Tabulated data for the cement clinker minerals (from Barin, Thermochemical Data of Pure Substances) and curated values for plastics.
  • Laliberté electrolyte-solution model for aqueous solutions such as sodium hydroxide in water.

The Property page lists what can be looked up and what each value reads when no data exist.

The data source sheet

On the Property results, tap the ⓘ beside a value (Phase, Density, specific heat capacity, vapor pressure, enthalpy of vaporization, enthalpy and Gibbs energy of formation, viscosity, thermal conductivity, surface tension or electrical conductivity). A sheet headed "Where this value comes from" opens, titled after the value, such as Density source. It shows:

Row What it says
Substance The chemical and the state the value is for, such as "Ethanol, liquid at 20.0 °C".
Method The correlation or model that gave the value, such as "CoolProp HEOS (reference equation of state)".
Library Which data library that method belongs to (see Where the data come from).
Valid range The temperature range the method was fitted over, in your temperature unit, or "not recorded for this method".

Where a range is known, a line under it says whether your temperature is inside it. Outside, the line is amber: the value is extrapolated.

A note at the foot says what kind of value it is. Most say the value comes from a correlation, or a model, fitted to measured data for this chemical rather than a generalized estimate. The enthalpy and Gibbs energy of formation also give their reference condition: 298.15 K (25 °C) and 1 bara, with each element in its standard reference state at zero, so these values do not change with the temperature and pressure you entered.

When a value is low-confidence, the sheet is titled with "accuracy" instead of "source", such as Density accuracy (Phase accuracy rather than Phase determination), and its icon and note are amber, saying what limits the value.

Changing a property's method

For vapor pressure, specific heat capacity, viscosity, thermal conductivity, enthalpy of vaporization and surface tension, the sheet ends with Change method when the libraries have more than one method for that chemical. Each method shows its library and valid range; methods whose range covers your temperature come first, and ranges your temperature falls outside are in amber. The method in use has a tick.

Tap another method and the properties are calculated again with it. Once you have picked one, Use the app's default goes back to the app's own choice. A picked method lasts until you look up a different substance, which starts from the app's own choices again; it is not part of a saved set.

Methods used

Most results end with a Methods used row: "See or change how this was calculated". Tap it to open a sheet listing what the result was calculated with. Where there is another method to pick, its choices sit under it as chips; tap one and the result is calculated again with it. Where there is nothing to pick, the sheet only says what was used.

Calculation What the sheet lists What you can change
Phase Equilibrium Equation of state For a single substance with a CoolProp reference equation: Reference equation (CoolProp) (the default) or Peng-Robinson cubic. A mixture is always solved with Peng-Robinson and fitted interaction parameters.
Chemical Equilibrium Method (Gibbs energy minimisation over the allowed species) and Species data, the source of each species' data Nothing; what changes the answer is which species are allowed
Reaction, Electrochemical Reaction Method (ΔH, ΔG and K from each species' formation data at the reaction temperature) and Thermochemical data, one record per species Nothing; each species has one record
Heater, Heat Exchanger Heat capacity for each phase of each substance (on the Heat Exchanger, of the hot and the cold stream) The heat-capacity correlation for that phase, where there is more than one. A mixture uses ideal mixing of each component's own correlations, with nothing to pick.
Vessel Heater Equation of state for the substance Reference equation (CoolProp) or Peng-Robinson cubic, for a substance with a reference equation

The Fluidized Bed has no Methods used row, and on Property each value's method is changed from its own ⓘ sheet instead.

The reference equation is the most accurate where it exists; Peng-Robinson is the general cubic the app uses for everything else. Picking Peng-Robinson for a substance with a reference equation is a way to see how much the choice of model moves the answer. Like a method picked on Property, a pick here is not part of a saved set.
