Thermo Tab

About

Four ways of asking what happens to a substance, each chosen from a card on the Choose a mode screen: phase changes, chemical equilibrium, a known reaction, and an electrochemical reaction.

Phase Equilibrium, "Heat, cool, compress or flash a fluid and see the phases it ends in": one chemical or mixture changing state, heated, cooled, compressed, expanded or throttled. Give the amount as a quantity for a sealed vessel or as a rate for a flowing stream, and the basis decides which. Finds the final state with the heat and work.

Chemical Equilibrium, "What a feed turns into at a temperature and pressure, by Gibbs energy minimization": a feed left to find its own minimum. You name the components; the app lists every compound their elements could form, you choose which may take part, and it finds the composition with the lowest total Gibbs energy. This is the mode for calcination, combustion products or ore reduction, where you do not already know what forms.

Reaction, "Balance an equation and get ΔH, ΔG, K and the duty": a reaction you already know. Name the reactants and products and the app balances the equation itself, applies your excess and conversion, and reports the full material balance with ΔH, ΔG and K.

Electrochemical Reaction, "Cell voltage, current and power for an electrolysis": the same reaction, driven by electricity or driving it. Adds the electrons transferred, counted from the balanced equation, the cell voltage, the electrical energy, the heat and, for a flow, the current of a monopolar or bipolar stack.

The difference between Chemical Equilibrium and Reaction is who decides the products: Chemical Equilibrium works them out, Reaction takes them from you.

Choosing a mode

The tab opens on the Choose a mode screen, with one card per mode. Tap a card and that mode's inputs open, with its name in the header and a ‹ back arrow that returns to the cards. The Docs ↗ pill opens the page for the mode you are in, or this page from the cards.

Every mode keeps what you typed while you move between them, so going back to the cards and into another mode never loses anything. The values in each mode come back when the app starts too, but the tab itself always opens on the cards.

Tapping the Thermo tab again steps back one screen at a time: from a result to its inputs, and from the inputs to the cards.

Inputs and results

Each mode's inputs are grouped in cards, and Calculate is pinned at the bottom of the screen. Choices such as Phase Equilibrium's Held constant are chips or switches rather than dropdowns, and a number field carries its unit in a menu inside it. That unit starts on the one chosen on the Units tab and follows it until you pick another in the field; from then on the field keeps its own unit. Changing a field's unit converts what is typed.

The answer opens on its own results screen:

  • An Inputs strip repeats what was calculated. Edit goes back to the inputs.
  • A headline card gives the answer first, such as the work on the fluid or how much of a feed reacts, with a sentence saying what happened.
  • Tiles show the next most important values, followed by cards of rows grouped by subject, with tables and charts where the mode has them.
  • Notes and caveats come next, then "Solved with", naming the method or data behind the result.
  • Methods used at the foot opens a sheet listing the methods behind the result, and lets you change them where there is a choice; the result is then calculated again. In Phase Equilibrium, for example, a single substance can be solved with CoolProp's reference equation of state or with Peng-Robinson. See Methods and sources.

Every value shows its unit beside it. Tap the unit to see that value in another unit: the change applies to that one value while the app is open, and never to the Units tab.

The In/Out table

Phase Equilibrium (for a mixture), Chemical Equilibrium, Reaction and Electrochemical Reaction show their compositions in the same table. Above it, a row of menus sets the units: Share (mol % or mass %), Amount (a mole or mass unit, or a flow unit for a stream) and, in Chemical Equilibrium's gas phase, Partial p (an absolute pressure unit). The table then has an In and an Out column pair, each a share and an amount, with the partial pressure last where it is shown. Some tables end with a Total row.

A trace share reads "<0.01" and a very small amount is written as a power of ten, such as 1.3·10⁻⁶, so the columns fit a phone. The units you pick in a table's menus stay with that table while the app is open.

Errors and warnings

Editing any input clears the answer, and changing a unit, here or on the Units tab, sends the mode back to its inputs. A result is never left on screen under inputs that no longer match it. Changing a unit on the Units tab converts the values in every mode, including the ones not on screen, so a mode you return to is already in the right units.

A wrong or unreadable input is reported in a red "Error:" banner on the inputs screen, naming the field, and the inputs stay up so you can fix it. Amber banners on a result are caveats worth reading: a phase-change warning, an extrapolated correlation, a mixture model's limits, a species left out for want of data.

Mixtures and chemical names

Wherever a mode asks for a chemical, it takes what the Property tab takes: a name, formula, CAS number or synonym, with matching suggestions as you type. Where a mode accepts a mixture, the Single substance / Mixture switch and the mixture rows work as on the Property tab. Phase Equilibrium also lets a mixture be given as each component's own amount, through Amounts under Given as.

Saving your inputs

The ⋯ menu in the header acts on the mode you are in, not on the whole tab. Save… stores that mode's inputs under a name of up to 40 characters, up to 25 sets per mode, either In the app or in Files / iCloud as a file; saving under an existing name replaces it. The mode's sets saved in the app are listed under Recent at the top of the menu, and Open a saved set… lists them all. Reset to defaults returns only that mode to its original inputs, so resetting Reaction leaves Phase Equilibrium as you left it, and it leaves your saved sets untouched. A file holds one mode's inputs and opens only in that mode. On the Choose a mode screen there is no mode to save or open, so Save… and Open a saved set… ask you to choose a calculation first, and Reset to defaults resets every mode. See Saving and opening.
