Vessel Heater

About

A sealed rigid vessel is heated or cooled. Finds the heat needed and the resulting pressure, phase and liquid level, and flags the liquid-full overpressure hazard.

What This Models

A rigid, sealed vessel whose contents are heated or cooled — fire exposure, solar gain, steam-out, loss of cooling, or an ambient swing. The vessel does not vent and does not change volume, so both the total volume and the mass inside are constant and the molar volume is fixed.

Inputs

The form opens with a Set-up picture of the vessel with its contents, the starting temperature ("now"), the target temperature, and the mass inside. It fills in as you type, with a ? for the final temperature when you give the heat instead. Below it the form is grouped Fluid, Vessel, Contents and Heating / cooling. Every number has its own unit menu inside its field.

Fluid is a Single substance / Mixture switch. A single substance goes in Substance, which opens on Propane. A mixture is built the same way as on the Property tab, with its shares given as Mole % or Mass %, or as Amounts: each component's own mass, in which case Mass in vessel steps aside and the mass is their sum.

Vessel is a Volume / Dimensions switch. Volume asks for one Vessel volume, 1000 L by default. Dimensions asks for:

  • Inside diameter (1 m) and Length (tangent to tangent) (2 m), side by side. The length is the straight shell only, excluding the heads, as its ⓘ says.
  • The head type, a switch of Elliptical (2:1), Hemispherical or Flat. Each head adds π·D³/24, π·D³/12 or nothing to the shell's π·D²·L/4.
  • The orientation, Vertical or Horizontal. It is recorded and does not change the volume or pressure here, but it sets how liquid level, wetted area and fill relate, which matters for relief and fire calculations. The results repeat this in amber.

A flat-headed vessel needs a non-zero length, or its volume would be zero.

Contents is a Mass / Pressure switch. Give a Mass in vessel (100 kg) and the app solves the initial state from it, or give an Initial pressure (10 bar) and the mass is back-calculated from the equation of state. Initial temperature is 20 °C, and sits beside the initial pressure when that is what you give.

Heating / cooling is a Final temperature / Heat added switch. Final temperature asks for the temperature to reach (80 °C) and finds the heat. Heat added asks for Heat added (negative to cool) and finds the temperature reached; as the note under it says, use a negative number to cool.

Press Calculate, pinned at the bottom of the screen. Editing any input, or changing a unit, clears the answer, and you are back at the inputs.

What comes back

The results open on their own screen. At the top an Inputs strip sums up what was calculated ("Propane · 100 kg · 20 °C → 80 °C"), and Edit takes you back to the form.

The answer comes first, in a dark card: Heat to add, with a sentence saying what the phase did, such as "Stays liquid+gas the whole way" or "Liquid+Gas at the start, liquid at the end". Tap the unit beside the number to change it. Under it is a picture of the vessel, labelled with its final phase, beside a thermometer running from the initial to the final temperature.

Three tiles follow: Final temp., Final phase, and Per kg, the heat divided by the mass in the vessel. Then the values, grouped:

  • Vessel: the Vessel volume, and for a vessel given by dimensions the Shell and the Heads (2) separately. Then the Contents (the mass inside) and its Moles.
  • Initial state and Final state, each with Temperature, Pressure, Phase and Vapour fraction ("single phase" if there is only one), and the Liquid volume, Vapour volume and Liquid fill as a percentage of the vessel. Liquid fill turns amber at 99.5 % or more.
  • Energy: Heat added (or Heat removed when cooling), and the Pressure rise between the two states.

Every value with a unit has its own unit menu; changing one changes only that row.

Amber warnings appear under the values. LIQUID-FULL fires when heating removes the vapour space that was there at the start; see The Liquid-Full Warning. A final pressure above 1000 bara is flagged as far beyond ordinary equipment, with a reminder that the equation of state is being extrapolated. For a mixture, a warning says the liquid and vapour volume split uses bubble- and dew-point molar volumes that sit at different pressures (the temperature glide), so treat it as an approximation. An estimated or extrapolated density is called out too, because the mass in the vessel comes straight from it.

Solved with: names the equation of state that produced the states. Methods used at the foot opens a sheet showing it; for a single substance that has a reference equation, you can switch between Reference equation (CoolProp) and Peng-Robinson cubic, and the case calculates again. See Methods and Sources.

Method

Constant volume and constant mass fix the molar volume; the final state then follows from the first law with no work term, in either direction.

Key Relations

V_m = V/n                    fixed, from the constant volume and mass
Q = n·(U₂ − U₁)               final temperature given -> duty found
U₂ = U₁ + Q/n                 duty given -> target U, then T solved

V_m is the molar volume, fixed for the whole calculation. Given a final temperature, the final state is a T,V_m flash and the duty follows directly. Given a duty instead, the target internal energy U₂ is known and the final temperature is found by root-finding on that same T,V_m flash.

The two-phase region is handled explicitly rather than left to the equation of state: a plain T,V_m flash inside the saturation dome puts the liquid under tension and returns a negative pressure, so the saturation envelope is checked first and a two-phase state is solved at the vapour fraction x satisfying x·V_m,g + (1−x)·V_m,l = V_m — the fraction that reproduces the vessel's own molar volume.

The Liquid-Full Warning

When heating drives the vapour fraction to zero, the vessel is liquid-full. Past that point there is no compressible vapour space left, and pressure rises almost vertically with temperature — a few degrees more can mean hundreds of bara. The calculation still reports a number, but it is extremely sensitive to both the temperature and the accuracy of the liquid density, so treat it as an indication that relief is required, not as a design pressure.

Limitations

The vessel is rigid (no elastic expansion of the shell, which in reality relieves a little of the hydraulic pressure rise), perfectly sealed, and uniform in temperature. Vessel wall heat capacity is not included, so a given duty heats the contents faster than it would in reality. There is no relief device — this is the unrelieved case, which is exactly what you size relief against.
